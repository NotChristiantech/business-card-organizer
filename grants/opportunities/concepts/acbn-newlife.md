# Three concepts: ACBN + NewLife Project Inc.

Not tied to one funder yet. Each is built from what NewLife **actually does**
(`../../orgs/new-life-project.md`) crossed with ACBN's assets, and each targets a
different funder set.

**The partnership's core logic:** ACBN has national reach, capital, research and
a federal track record but is English-only, Ontario-centred, and not a women's
organization. NewLife is bilingual, Ottawa-based, women-focused and West
Africa-connected but smaller. Each covers the other's gap almost exactly.

---

## Concept 1 — Bilingual national women's entrepreneurship

**The unlock, not just a project.** Federal pan-Canadian streams routinely
require delivery in **both official languages**. That single requirement is why
the WAGE application was scoped to the $1M regional band rather than $1.4M
pan-Canadian. NewLife delivers in French.

With NewLife carrying French delivery and ACBN's 40+ partner organizations
carrying national reach, the consortium can credibly claim pan-Canadian scope for
the first time — on this and every future federal application.

**The project:** a bilingual national program for Black women entrepreneurs,
combining the NewLife Women's World Forum model with ACBN's business development
and capital access, delivered in English and French across at least four regions.

**Funders:** WAGE (next cycle), ISED, ESDC pan-Canadian streams, Canadian
Heritage official languages programs.

**Why it wins:** it answers the women's-organization priority through NewLife,
the Black-economy mandate through ACBN, and the official-languages requirement
that eliminates most competitors. Very few Black-led organizations can claim all
three.

**Check first:** how much French-language delivery capacity NewLife genuinely
has beyond tutoring, and what "four WAGE regions" requires in practice.

---

## Concept 2 — Canada–West Africa women's trade corridor

**The most distinctive thing this partnership could do.** NewLife runs programs
in **Sierra Leone, Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire** and holds a Carleton University
placement partnership for the **Queen Elizabeth Scholars Advanced Scholars West
Africa Program**. ACBN has the business development, capital and convening side.

ACBN's own policy review recommends government *"capitalise on diasporan
connections to create business partnerships and explore trade opportunities,
especially with African and Caribbean countries"*. This is the chance to
demonstrate it rather than recommend it.

**The project:** build a trade and enterprise corridor connecting Black
women-owned businesses in Canada with counterparts and supply chains in West
Africa — market access, export readiness, diaspora investment.

**Funders:** Global Affairs Canada, CanExport, ISED, National Trade Corridors
Fund (ACBN already has a Drive folder for it), Canadian Heritage.

**Why it wins:** almost no Canadian organization can credibly deliver
Canada–West Africa women's enterprise work with existing operations on both
sides. It also moves ACBN into trade and international development funding,
which is a different envelope from the community funding it competes in now.

**Check first:** whether ACBN wants to be in international development at all —
it is a genuinely new direction with its own compliance burden.

---

## Concept 3 — Newcomer women: language to enterprise

**The most fundable of the three, and the most conventional.** NewLife delivers
French and English training to newcomers *"to improve their economic capacity"*,
plus vocational skills and internships. ACBN delivers business development and
microloans.

Together that is a complete pathway newcomers currently have to assemble
themselves: **arrival → language → skills → self-employment → capital**.

**The project:** an integrated pathway for newcomer Black women, from language
acquisition through vocational skills to business registration and financing —
with NewLife's sewing and crafts training and NewLife Designs as the working
proof that skills training can become an income-generating enterprise.

**Funders:** IRCC settlement funding, ESDC, Ontario newcomer and employment
programs, Ontario Trillium Foundation, United Way.

**Why it wins:** it addresses a documented problem — newcomer women concentrated
in low-wage work — with an evidenced pathway rather than a proposal. It also maps
directly onto the WAGE priority of *"wage gaps and job concentration in
lower-paid roles"*, so it is reusable when that fund reopens.

**Check first:** whether the funder considers this service delivery. WAGE would
(ineligible there); IRCC and ESDC fund exactly this.

---

## Recommendation

**Concept 3 to fund the work, Concept 1 to change what ACBN can apply for.**

Concept 3 is the most conventionally fundable and sits inside what both
organizations already do. But Concept 1 is worth more long-term: bilingual
delivery capacity is an eligibility asset that raises the ceiling on every
federal application ACBN makes, not just one. If NewLife's French capacity is
real, establishing it formally is the highest-leverage thing this partnership
can do.

Concept 2 is the most distinctive and the biggest stretch. Worth pursuing only
if ACBN actively wants an international portfolio.
