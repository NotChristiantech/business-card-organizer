# NewLife Project Inc.

> **Correction.** This record was a stub saying "details not yet recorded", and
> on that basis the WAGE application record concluded ACBN had no
> women's-organization partner. That was wrong. NewLife is substantively a
> women's organization, is bilingual, and is based in Ottawa — three assets that
> materially change what this partnership can go after.

## Who they are

Founded **1994 in Sierra Leone**, registered in **Canada in 2014**. A
**bilingual not-for-profit operating in English and French**, based in
**Ottawa**. Founder and Executive Director **Brenda E. Williams**, an honoree of
**100 Accomplished Black Canadian Women (2020)**.

Mission: *"to see women and children rise to a place of divine accomplishment by
fulfilling their purpose, rebuilding their broken lives, and attaining total
restoration."*

## Why this is a women's organization

Stated purposes include *"to empower women by conducting seminars, discussion
forums to provide tools, resources and information on how to start a business
(entrepreneurship)... both in North America and West Africa"*, delivered through
the **NewLife Women's World Forum**.

Their women's forums are described as *"a safe space to discuss issues affecting
women including gender and social issues"*.

For any funder prioritising women's organizations — WAGE above all — **NewLife
is the credential ACBN lacks**. Lead with Brenda, the Women's World Forum, and
the constitutional purpose, not with ACBN's percentage of women members.

## The three assets that change ACBN's options

**1. Bilingual — English and French.** Many federal pan-Canadian funding streams
require delivery in **both official languages**, which is the requirement that
forced the WAGE application down to the $1M regional band instead of $1.4M
pan-Canadian. NewLife can carry the French side. This is the single most
valuable thing in this partnership.

**2. Ottawa.** Federal proximity, and a second Ontario region. Note that ACBN's
current FedDev agreement funds **net new offices in Ajax, Ottawa and Hamilton** —
so the two organizations are about to be in the same city.

**3. West Africa.** Active programs in **Sierra Leone, Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire**,
plus a placement partnership with **Carleton University** for the **Queen
Elizabeth Scholars — Advanced Scholars West Africa Program**.

ACBN's own policy review recommends government *"capitalise on diasporan
connections to create business partnerships and explore trade opportunities,
especially with African and Caribbean countries"*
(`../library/research/evidence-base.md`). NewLife is a live example of exactly
that — ACBN can stop recommending it and start demonstrating it.

## What they actually deliver

- **NewLife Women's World Forum** — women's empowerment forums and workshops
- **Vocational training** — sewing and crafts, explicitly *"designed to build
  useful sewing skills to improve income and economic status"*
- **Mother-Daughter sewing sessions** — intergenerational skills transfer
- **NewLife Designs** — an income-generating entrepreneurial division, with
  proceeds partly directed to charity. A working social enterprise
- **French and English tutoring** for newcomers, framed as economic capacity
- **Internships** with Adler University, Centennial College, École Alternative
  and the Youth Service Bureau
- **Youth mentorship**, ages 15–21
- **International relief** to Sierra Leone, Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire

## History with ACBN

- **$21,000 SBCCI** secured by ACBN on their behalf
- **Black Opportunity Fund** application written for them, March 2022 (invoiced
  through The Centre for Strategic Impact and Growth, $565)
- Ryan referred them to an English/French training grant, June 2026
- Ryan provided a **letter of support for their Black Youth Action Plan
  application**, September 2026 — Economic Empowerment Program, Entrepreneurship
  Stream, Ministry of Citizenship and Multiculturalism

A genuine multi-year working relationship, not a nominal partnership. That is
what a reviewer assessing consortium risk wants to see.

## Stated priorities going forward

From their own deck: continue impact for women and girls; establish a
comprehensive **Youth Empowerment Program for at-risk youth in Ottawa**;
increase membership and partnerships; collaborate with other socially-oriented
organizations.

Any joint application should serve their stated direction, not only ACBN's.
