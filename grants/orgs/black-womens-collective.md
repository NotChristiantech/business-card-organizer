# Black Women's Collective

**A for-profit business** (confirmed by Ryan, 2026-09-04).

## Not eligible as a grant applicant

WAGE requires every applicant to be a **not-for-profit organization**. BWC
therefore cannot be lead applicant or co-applicant on the Women's Economic and
Leadership Opportunities Fund
(`../applications/wage-womens-economic-leadership-2026.md`).

This is a general constraint, not a WAGE quirk: most federal and foundation
grant programs restrict applicants to not-for-profits. Assume BWC is ineligible
as an applicant anywhere unless a specific program says otherwise.

## What BWC can still do

1. **Named collaborator with a letter of support.** No money changes hands, no
   eligibility question arises. This is the clean option and it is worth having —
   BWC is a Black women-owned business, which is the population the project
   exists to serve, so its endorsement is evidence of authentic connection rather
   than an organization speaking on someone's behalf.
2. **Advisory role** — sitting on a project steering group, so the businesses
   affected by the criteria being rewritten have a voice in rewriting them. That
   is good GBA Plus practice (`../library/gba-plus.md`) and reads well.
3. **Contracted service provider**, with care. Federal funders scrutinise
   contracts awarded to a for-profit named in the application, and rules
   commonly limit private profit from grant funds. If BWC is to be paid,
   establish fair market value and a defensible procurement process, and check
   the funder's position first.

## Do not

- List BWC as an applicant or co-applicant
- Route grant funds to BWC without confirming the funder permits it
