# Afro-Caribbean Business Network Foundation (ACBN)

## Mission

The Afro-Caribbean Business Network was created to provide a resource to business
owners in our community to help them succeed. Running a business can be difficult
on your own — with more support, entrepreneurship gets easier.

## Four pillars

- **Entrepreneurship** — fostering the next generation of Black Canadian
  entrepreneurs with programs and services.
- **Convening** — connecting the community through the means of technology.
- **Access to Funding** — providing microloan funding and access to investments,
  with the tools and skills to build financial literacy.
- **Economic Inclusion** — breaking the economic barriers for the
  underrepresented.

## Programs and vehicles

- **ACBN Microloan** — $500 to $25,000 direct lending to Black entrepreneurs.
- **Grant writing services** — ACBN writes applications on behalf of other
  community organizations (see `orgs/` for the ones tracked here). This is both
  a revenue line and a community-capacity strategy, and it means most
  applications in this system have an `applicant` that is *not* ACBN.
- **Housing** — a distinct workstream (`ryan.housing@acbncanada.com`), running
  against CMHC, the NL Community Housing Growth Fund and Vancity Community
  Foundation. Different funders, different evidence base, different deadlines
  than the entrepreneurship work.

## Positioning that has worked

ACBN's strongest case to a funder is **intermediary reach**: we don't just serve
our own members, we hold relationships with 40+ Black-focused organizations and
have moved money to them (see `library/track-record.md` — IRP funding to ResQ
Youth and Custodia, SBCCI funding to New Life Project). Funders trying to reach
Black communities at scale cannot do it one organization at a time; ACBN is the
distribution layer. Lead with that when the program is ecosystem- or
capacity-focused.
