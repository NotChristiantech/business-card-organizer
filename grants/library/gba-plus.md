# GBA Plus

**Gender-based Analysis Plus** is the Government of Canada's required analytical
framework for assessing how a policy, program or initiative affects different
groups of people. **WAGE owns GBA Plus** for the federal government — so when a
WAGE call points applicants at the CSPS course
(`https://opn.csps-efpc.gc.ca/viewer/INC101-EN`), it is signalling that the
application will be assessed on GBA Plus thinking.

Treat it as a scored requirement, not a reading suggestion.

## What the "Plus" means, and why it matters enormously here

GBA Plus is **not** analysis of women as a single group. The Plus is the whole
point: sex and gender **intersecting** with other identity factors — race,
ethnicity, Indigenous identity, disability, income, age, language, immigration
status, geography, education.

**This reframes ACBN's position on gender-equality funding.**

The WAGE application record previously warned that this was a mandate-adjacent
application — a gender agenda ACBN would have to translate its work into, which
is the category with a near-perfect record of failing
(`win-loss-analysis.md`).

Under GBA Plus that warning weakens considerably. An organization working with
**Black women entrepreneurs** is not translating its work into a gender agenda.
It is performing precisely the intersectional analysis the department's own
framework demands. A project addressing gender *without* race would be the weaker
GBA Plus submission, not the stronger one.

The warning does not disappear — WAGE still **prioritizes women's
organizations**, which is a question of applicant identity rather than analytical
fit. But the analytical objection is answered.

## ACBN's strongest GBA Plus argument

GBA Plus depends on **disaggregated data**, and every source in
`research/evidence-base.md` says the same thing: disaggregated data on Black
Canadians barely exists. That is usually cited as a limitation. For ACBN it is a
strength, because ACBN is one of the few organizations actively closing the gap:

- Its own survey of **1,014 Black founders** in Peel and the GTA
- A **Rapid Labour Market Needs Assessment conducted with the Diversity, Policy
  and Planning Branch of the Ontario Public Service**
- Microloan adjudication records for a population mainstream credit scoring
  rejects

So the GBA Plus section can say something almost no other applicant can: *the
analysis you require cannot currently be done properly for this population,
because the data does not exist — and here is our primary research filling part
of that gap.*

That converts a sector-wide weakness into ACBN's differentiator.

## How to use it in an application

1. **Name the intersecting factors explicitly** — gender, race, immigration
   status, income, geography (Peel/GTA), business size, language.
2. **Disaggregate every claim.** Not "entrepreneurs face barriers" but "Black
   women founders of micro-enterprises in Peel face *these* barriers", with the
   evidence.
3. **Show differential impact.** Who benefits, who does not, and who might be
   left further behind — GBA Plus rewards naming limitations honestly.
4. **Identify data gaps as findings**, with a plan to address them. This is the
   move that plays to ACBN's strength.
5. **Apply it to the project itself**, not only the problem — who is in the room
   when decisions are made, and whose evidence counts.
