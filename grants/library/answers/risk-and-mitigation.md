# Risk and mitigation

Reusable near-verbatim. The reason it works: every mitigation is a **named
recurring activity**, not an intention. "We will monitor closely" scores nothing;
"weekly check-ins, monthly reporting, quarterly strategy meetings" scores.

| Risk factor | Mitigating strategy |
|---|---|
| **Cost** — marketing, labour, material, accounting, legal | Feasibility study on fixed and variable costs; cost allocations made from the Budget Plan |
| **Resources** — partners with the expertise to coach and mentor | Focus group with experts in financial planning, document checking, coaching, program management, financial assessment and approval, and relationship building |
| **Industry / market competition** — new entrants and dominant competitors | Monthly workshop programs reinforcing strategies for sustainability, profitability and competitive edge |
| **Business assessment criteria** — screening and processing methods | Info sessions with applicants to review business plans and cash flow projections, make recommendations, and provide coaching and training |
| **Partners not fulfilling commitments** | Weekly check-ins, monthly reporting, quarterly strategy meetings against outlined goals. Coaching for partners falling behind; if mutually agreed they cannot deliver, ACBN staff take over that region's work |
| **Operational** — information flow, approvals, follow-ups | Project Manager/Coordinator manages end-to-end procedures; weekly progress reports; quarterly surveys; time-based process calendar with delay alerts; cohort portal |
| **Socio-economic (macro)** — policy change, regulation, pandemics, inflation | Weekly bulletins/newsletters with updates; change management workshops for members |

## Adapting it

Trim to the risks the funder's own program actually carries. A wage-subsidy
program does not need the partner-failure row; a multi-partner ecosystem grant
needs it most of all.

The partner-failure row is the strongest one in the table and the one most worth
keeping. Naming what happens when a partner fails — ACBN absorbs the work — is
the thing that makes a reviewer believe the other six.
