# Theory of change

**This is ACBN's actual intellectual position, taken verbatim from the concept
paper behind the $1.315M Ecosystem Fund award.** It is not a reconstruction. Use
it as the spine of any application that asks why ACBN rather than a mainstream
provider — it is the most distinctive thing in the entire library.

## The argument

When society talks about the systemic barriers and racism that Black
entrepreneurs face, we see the Eurocentric cultural ethos that is deeply rooted
in our society. Individualism, materialism, capitalism — these Eurocentric
experiences relegate the African diaspora to the periphery.

In contrast, the African diaspora — Afro-Canadian, African American,
Afro-Caribbean, or someone directly from the African continent — and their
Africentric culture hold a profound sense of spirituality and collective
existence.

In a society where Western cultural values dominate, the African diaspora do not
have the same means and resources precisely because of this cultural gap. We hear
about barriers accessing start-up financing. While financial institutions treat
all applications equally, the systemic barrier lies in **inequity as opposed to
inequality**.

ACBN sees **culture and economics as the dual instruments** necessary to bridge
the gap we call systemic racism. One without the other perpetuates the
disenfranchisement of African people in Canadian society. In the
economic/entrepreneurship sector, there are no services that address the
fundamental cultural divide.

ACBN's singular focus is to enable Black entrepreneurs to achieve economic
success **using an Afrocentric paradigm**.

## Why it works on a reviewer

Three things, worth understanding rather than just copying:

1. **"Inequity as opposed to inequality"** does real work in one phrase. It
   pre-empts the reviewer's likely objection — banks treat everyone the same —
   and reframes it as the problem rather than the defence.
2. **It names a gap no competitor fills.** Generic entrepreneur centres and even
   other Black business organizations offer services; ACBN claims the cultural
   frame itself is the missing instrument. That is a category argument, not a
   capacity argument, and capacity arguments are what everyone else submits.
3. **It is not a deficit narrative.** The community has a culture with
   something the mainstream lacks — collective existence — rather than a
   deficiency to be remediated. Federal Black-focused programs are written in
   this register and reviewers recognise it.

## Where to use it

Open with it on anything ecosystem-, capacity- or equity-focused. Compress it to
a paragraph for a needs statement; run it in full where there's a theory of
change or approach section. For a narrowly operational program (a wage subsidy
like Canada Summer Jobs), it is too much — use the plain mission text instead.
