# Voice and style guide

How ACBN applications should read. Derived from the deck, the grant tracker, and
Ryan's own correspondence — refine as we learn what individual funders respond to.

## Register

Direct, plain, confident. Ryan's own writing is short-sentenced and unfussy
("Grants are out there, let us get to work for you"). Applications should keep
that spine while meeting the formality a funder expects. What that rules out:

- **No inflated adjectives.** "Transformative," "unprecedented," "world-class."
  Reviewers discount them instantly. The numbers carry the weight.
- **No apology or hedging.** Not "we hope to be able to" — "we will."
- **No jargon for its own sake.** If "capacity building" can be "help
  organizations do more with the money they have," say that. Use sector terms
  where the funder's own guidelines use them, because reviewers score against a
  rubric written in those words.

## Structure of a strong answer

1. **Claim first.** Answer the actual question in the first sentence. Reviewers
   read hundreds of these; do not make them hunt.
2. **Evidence second.** A number from `track-record.md` or `reach-and-impact.md`.
3. **Mechanism third.** How it will actually happen — named activities, real
   people, a timeline.
4. **Stakes last.** What the community gets, in concrete terms.

## Recurring frames that work for ACBN

- **The distribution frame** — "You cannot reach Black entrepreneurs across
  Canada one organization at a time. ACBN already holds those relationships."
- **The graduated-trust frame** — from $500 to $1.3M, each award managed and
  reported on. Risk is already demonstrated as low.
- **The proximity frame** — ACBN is led by and embedded in the community it
  serves. This is not outreach to a population, it is the population organizing
  itself.
- **The both-sides frame** — ACBN both receives funding and moves it to others.
  Unusual, and it answers the "will this stay concentrated in one org" worry.

## Mirror the funder

Before drafting, read the funder's own program language and reuse their nouns.
If the guidelines say "underrepresented entrepreneurs," don't write
"marginalized business owners." Reviewers score against a rubric, and matching
the rubric's vocabulary is free points.

## Numbers discipline

Every figure traces to `library/track-record.md`, `library/reach-and-impact.md`,
or a cited document. If a number is in a record's `needs_verification` list, it
does not go into a submitted application. Getting caught with a soft number is
the fastest way to lose a funder permanently.
