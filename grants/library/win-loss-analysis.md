# Win/loss analysis

What the archive says about where ACBN wins and where it doesn't. This is the
thing the whole system exists to produce.

## Read this caveat first

**The win/loss counts below are not a win rate, and must never be quoted as one.**

`track-record.md` records **21 awards**, but only 8 have application records
here. Most wins survive only as line items in a deck; the declines were recorded
because Ryan listed them from memory in September 2026. Losses are therefore
over-represented and wins under-represented. Backfilling the awarded folders in
`corpus-index.md` would correct it.

Two things do survive the bias:

- **The dollar-weighted picture**, because the confirmed amounts are the large
  awards and they are verified.
- **Within-funder comparisons**, because those hold the sampling constant.

## The dominant pattern: mandate-explicit programs, weighted by dollars

Confirmed award amounts (September 2026) changed this analysis, and it is worth
being explicit about how.

**Every dollar of confirmed funding but $8,000 came from a program explicitly
about Black economic participation:**

| Program | Amount |
|---|---|
| FedDev BEP Ecosystem Fund, rounds one and two | $3,315,000 |
| SBCCI, 2020 and 2024 | $140,000 |
| FFBC BIG Bridge and Build | $75,000 |
| **Black-economy-explicit subtotal** | **$3,530,000** |
| ESDC Canada Summer Jobs, 2025 and 2026 | $8,000 |

Canada Summer Jobs is **0.2%** of confirmed funding.

Every decline, meanwhile, is a program where ACBN's work had to be translated
into someone else's agenda: crime prevention (twice), the Sustainable
Development Goals, a race relations event, generic youth employment.

`theory-of-change.md` predicts exactly this. ACBN's distinctive argument is the
Afrocentric economic frame — culture and economics as dual instruments. An
application that abandons that frame to fit an adjacent agenda competes on
capacity alone, where ACBN is one mid-sized nonprofit among many. Where the
program is *about* Black economic participation, ACBN is close to unbeatable:
FedDev funded it twice and raised the second award by 52%.

### The correction: counting applications misled me

An earlier version of this file led with a different pattern — that ACBN wins
*formula-assessed* programs and loses *competitively scored* ones, built on ESDC
having two wins (both Canada Summer Jobs) against three losses.

**That was an artifact of counting applications instead of weighting them.**
Canada Summer Jobs is a $4,000 wage subsidy for two students. The genuinely large
wins — BEP at $1.315M then $2M, SBCCI, FFBC — are all *competitively assessed*.
ACBN wins competitive programs routinely, and wins the biggest ones.

Two lessons, and the second is the durable one:

1. Counting applications treats a $4,000 wage subsidy and a $2,000,000 ecosystem
   fund as equal events. **Weight by dollars.**
2. This is the second pattern in this file to dissolve on contact with evidence
   (see the Vancity section below). Patterns here are hypotheses, not findings.

### What survives

The ESDC observation still holds in a narrower form: within ESDC, ACBN has won
only the mechanical program and lost all three discretionary ones. That is real,
but ESDC is a marginal funder by value, so it is a footnote rather than a
strategy.

**CIPAD remains the genuine anomaly** — mandate-explicit and declined. It is one
data point against a very strong dollar-weighted pattern, and ESDC did not answer
the debrief request, so it stays unexplained. Do not over-read a single decline
against $3.5M of confirmed wins in the same category.

## Public Safety Canada is 0-for-2

GCP-VOs ($500,000) and the National Crime Prevention Fund, both declined.

Two data points is thin, but they point the same way and one was a large ask.
The crime-prevention framing requires arguing that entrepreneurship is upstream
of criminal justice outcomes — a real argument, but one where ACBN competes
against organizations whose entire mandate is that causal chain.

**Recommendation: stop applying here** until either a decline reason suggests a
fixable weakness, or ACBN partners with an organization whose core work is
community safety. Ryan's time is the binding constraint, and this envelope has
consumed two applications for nothing.

## Vancity: neither theory was right, and that is the lesson

Vancity approved the **loan** ($150,000 pre-development) and declined the
**grant**, same funder, same period. This file previously advanced two
explanations in turn:

1. ACBN reads as a housing *operator*, not a *research institution*
2. ACBN is not a qualified donee, and foundations restrict grants to donees

**Both were wrong.** Vancity put the reason in writing: the grant proposal
(*Building the Blueprint*, a capacity-building playbook) fell outside their
current grant scope, which is focused on **pre-development activities**, and
their **capacity-building allocation for the year was already fully committed**.
The loan was a pre-development loan for Springbank — squarely inside scope.

Budget and scope. Not eligibility, not merit, not structure.

### Why this belongs in a win/loss file

It is the clearest evidence here for how to *use* this analysis. Two plausible,
mutually exclusive theories were constructed from the same two data points, each
implied a different and costly remedy — build research credibility, or pursue
charitable registration — and one email dissolved both.

**Inference from outcomes is for generating questions, not answers.** Every
pattern in this file is a hypothesis until a funder confirms it. Where a funder
will tell you (foundations usually will; ESDC did not answer on CIPAD), ask
before acting.

It also reclassifies the decline: an allocation that was spent is a **reapply
next cycle**, which makes this the most straightforwardly recoverable loss in the
archive.

## What to do

1. **Request decline reasons for the five not yet chased** — Public Safety
   ×2, CRRF, SDG, Youth Employment. Vancity answered in full and ESDC did not
   answer on CIPAD, so expect foundations to reply and federal programs not to.
2. **Public Safety is the one worth pressing**, being 0-for-2 including a $500K
   ask. The answer decides whether to abandon that envelope permanently.
3. **Treat BEP 2.0 reporting as the outcome-data programme.** It is the only
   route to the evidence competitive applications need.
4. **Check charitable status before every foundation application** (`eligibility.md`).
5. **Weight the pipeline hard toward mandate-explicit Black-economy programs.**
   This is where $3.53M of $3.538M confirmed funding came from. Applications
   requiring ACBN to translate its work into an adjacent agenda have a
   near-perfect record of failing, and they cost the same to write.
6. **Protect the FedDev relationship above all.** Two rounds, $3.315M, and a 52%
   increase. Nothing else in the portfolio is close, and BEP 2.0's reporting
   decides whether there is a round three.
7. **Right-size effort to value.** Canada Summer Jobs deserves a diary entry, not
   a strategy discussion. Judge every prospect on expected value, not on how
   winnable it looks.
8. **Backfill the awarded folders** from `corpus-index.md` so this rests on the
   full record rather than a sample skewed toward recently-recalled declines.
