# Win/loss analysis

What the archive says about where ACBN wins and where it doesn't. This is the
thing the whole system exists to produce.

## Read this caveat first

**The 6:7 ratio below is not a win rate, and must never be quoted as one.**

`track-record.md` records **16 awards**, but only 6 have application records
here. The old wins survive as line items in a deck; the declines were recorded
because Ryan listed them from memory in September 2026. So losses are
over-represented and wins under-represented. Backfilling the awarded folders in
`corpus-index.md` would correct it.

What survives the bias is the *pattern* of which kinds of applications land,
because that comparison is within-funder.

## Pattern 1 — formula programs win, competitively-scored ones don't

The sharpest signal in the archive, because it holds *within a single funder*.

At ESDC, ACBN has won **Canada Summer Jobs twice** and lost **CIPAD, the SDG
Funding Program, and Youth Employment**. Canada Summer Jobs is a wage subsidy
assessed against largely mechanical criteria. The other three are competitively
scored on narrative and outcome evidence.

Same department, same organization, opposite results — and the variable that
separates them is how the application is assessed.

**What this implies:** the constraint is not eligibility, relationships, or the
quality of ACBN's work. It is that competitively scored applications are won on
*demonstrated outcomes*, and that is precisely the gap documented in
`reach-and-impact.md`. ACBN can evidence reach superbly and outcomes barely at
all.

This is the strongest available argument for treating BEP 2.0 reporting as a
strategic priority rather than an administrative one. It is the mechanism that
produces the outcome data every competitive application currently lacks.

## Pattern 2 — mandate-explicit wins, mandate-adjacent loses

**Won** where the program is *explicitly about Black economic participation:*
BEP Ecosystem Fund (Black entrepreneurship), SBCCI (Black communities), FFBC
(Black communities).

**Lost** where ACBN's work had to be translated into an adjacent agenda: crime
prevention (twice), the Sustainable Development Goals, a race relations event,
generic youth employment.

`theory-of-change.md` predicts exactly this. ACBN's distinctive argument is the
Afrocentric economic frame — culture and economics as dual instruments. An
application that abandons that frame to fit someone else's competes on
capacity alone, where ACBN is one mid-sized nonprofit among many.

**The exception that matters:** **CIPAD was declined.** It supports the UN
International Decade for People of African Descent — as mandate-explicit as a
federal program gets. If Pattern 2 were the whole story, ACBN should have won
it. That it lost suggests Pattern 1 dominates: CIPAD is competitively scored,
and mandate fit does not rescue thin outcome evidence.

**Getting the CIPAD decline reason is the single highest-value follow-up in this
file.** It is the one case where fit is not in question, so the answer isolates
execution.

## Pattern 3 — Public Safety Canada is 0-for-2

GCP-VOs ($500,000) and the National Crime Prevention Fund, both declined.

Two data points is thin, but they point the same way and one was a large ask.
The crime-prevention framing requires arguing that entrepreneurship is upstream
of criminal justice outcomes — a real argument, but one where ACBN competes
against organizations whose entire mandate is that causal chain.

**Recommendation: stop applying here** until either a decline reason suggests a
fixable weakness, or ACBN partners with an organization whose core work is
community safety. Ryan's time is the binding constraint, and this envelope has
consumed two applications for nothing.

## Pattern 4 — Vancity: the likely reason is charitable status

Vancity approved the **loan** and declined the **research grant**, same funder,
same period.

An earlier hypothesis in `funders/vancity-community-foundation.md` was that ACBN
reads as a housing operator rather than a research institution. **There is now a
better explanation.**

The Articles of Amendment establish that ACBN is explicitly a **non-charitable**
not-for-profit. Foundations routinely restrict *grants* to registered charities
and qualified donees, while facing no such restriction on *loans*.

If that is the reason, the decline had nothing to do with the application's
quality, and the operator/researcher reading is a red herring that would have
sent ACBN off improving the wrong thing.

**Test it:** ask Vancity directly. The loan relationship makes it an easy
question, and the answer changes strategy across every foundation in the
pipeline. See `eligibility.md`.

## What to do

1. **Request decline reasons for all seven.** Ask specifically whether
   eligibility or charitable status was a factor. Most funders will answer;
   almost nobody asks. Seven reasons would be worth more than the next three
   applications.
2. **Prioritise CIPAD and Vancity** among those requests — they are the two that
   discriminate between competing explanations.
3. **Treat BEP 2.0 reporting as the outcome-data programme.** It is the only
   route to the evidence competitive applications need.
4. **Check charitable status before every foundation application** (`eligibility.md`).
5. **Weight the pipeline toward mandate-explicit, Black-economy programs**, and
   toward funders with a track record: FedDev, FFBC, SBCCI-type capacity funds.
6. **Backfill the awarded folders** from `corpus-index.md` so this analysis rests
   on the full record rather than a skewed sample.
