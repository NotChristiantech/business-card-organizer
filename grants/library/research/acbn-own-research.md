# ACBN's own research

The most under-used asset in this whole system. ACBN has produced **three**
pieces of original work — most organizations of its size have none, and it
changes what ACBN can claim to be.

## 1. ACBN entrepreneur survey — 1,014 respondents

Black founders in Peel and the GTA. Findings:

- **70%** in traditional service-based, low-tech companies
- **71%** either unregistered or sole proprietors

The second figure powered the argument that Black businesses were structurally
excluded from the **$962M Regional Recovery Relief Fund**, which restricted
support to incorporated firms. A documented case of a neutral rule producing an
inequitable outcome — the concrete illustration of the "inequity, not
inequality" argument in `../theory-of-change.md`.

Raw results: `https://drive.google.com/file/d/1IhByLhOQc9T00j-GMlAWGgS9g6BB1HWm/view`

## 2. ACBN Rapid Labour Market Needs Assessment (Feb 2023)

**Conducted in coordination with the Diversity, Policy and Planning Branch of
the Ontario Public Service.**

That clause is the credential. It is not a self-published community report — it
is research ACBN did *with the Ontario government*. For any Ontario application
(OTF above all), it is evidence that the province has already treated ACBN as a
credible research partner. Lead with it there.

Findings:
- Black businesses face systemic barriers: limited access to capital,
  mentorship, and networks
- There is a lack of data on Black-owned businesses in Ontario, making effective
  policy design difficult
- Policymakers need a more targeted approach to supporting Black entrepreneurship

Recommendations: use Black data firms to collect and analyse data on Black
businesses; partner with online platforms to promote Black-owned businesses.

## 3. Gibbs & Knight — policy response (Sept 2024)

Errol A. Gibbs and **Ryan O'Neil Knight**, responding to two national reports on
Black entrepreneurship. Ryan is a published co-author of a policy critique, which
is a different kind of standing from being a service provider.

The argument: the reports' focus on **capital financing as the primary solution
is too narrow.**

- Capital is necessary but not sufficient. Business growth, sustainability, and
  actual market demand matter as much.
- The Black Entrepreneurial Economic Ecosystem (BEEE) lacks an **"industrial
  mindset"** — project management skills, resources, infrastructure.
- Black businesses must be **scaled to qualify as suppliers** to government and
  corporate procurement.
- Proposes a **Canadian Black Empowerment Initiative (CBEI)**: investment in
  physical and digital infrastructure, Black data collection, Black consortiums,
  and Black "think tanks."

### Where this is powerful, and where it is a risk

**Powerful:** it positions ACBN as thinking about the sector, not just operating
in it. Where a funder asks about the wider ecosystem or systemic change, this is
a real answer with ACBN's name on it.

**Risky:** it is a critique of the prevailing view that capital is the answer —
and some funders in this space *are* capital programs. Do not lead with it in an
application to a loan fund or a financing instrument. Deploy where the funder is
asking about systems, strategy, or gaps.

## What this collectively lets ACBN claim

That it is a **research-informed practitioner**: it runs programs, surveys its
own community, has done commissioned work with the Ontario Public Service, and
publishes policy analysis. That combination is rare and should appear in the
organizational capacity answer, not just the needs statement.
